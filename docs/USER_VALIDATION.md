# Target-user validation kit

Status: protocol prepared; no interviews or usability sessions claimed. Use fictional data; no insurance identifiers or real medical records are needed.

## Recruit and consent

Ask an adult unfamiliar with US healthcare whether they will try a 10-minute prototype task. Explain that this is not care advice, record notes only with consent, and do not collect names or health details. Obtain separate consent for any quote, screen recording or public attribution. Stop if they decline.

## Three tasks

1. In the NEU / 02445 example, identify whom you would contact next and what you still need to confirm.
2. Explain which displayed amounts are annual plan fees, personal estimates, fictional examples or unknowns.
3. Add a provided fictional candidate, record an office reply, leave the page and resume the task from home.

Do not coach before noting the initial attempt. Ask “what would you do next?” and “what makes you think that?” rather than “is this useful?”

## Record per session

- Anonymous session ID, date and consent scope.
- Task: completed independently / with help / not completed; time and specific blocker.
- Participant's explanation of next action, source, uncertainty and external sharing.
- Any false inference: presumed network, quote, booking, diagnosis or guaranteed savings.
- Verbatim quote only if consented; otherwise paraphrase and label it.
- Change proposed, issue severity and whether retesting resolves it.

## Acceptance

Every observed participant should be able to identify a next action and distinguish an unknown cost from free care. Treat any mistaken confirmed-network, booking or safety inference as a release issue. Report sample size and failures; a small convenience sample does not establish product-market fit.

## Separate buyer discovery

Ask a university/student-support representative about current navigation work, repeated questions, ownership, distribution, procurement and budget. Do not turn willingness to discuss into willingness to pay.
